Timing

How long each stage takes

Measured across all three client documents. The stages are a genuine sequence, so they are numbered.

  1. 01Identify the file195–544 ms Five scripter conventions decide it by rule. No model call.
  2. 02Open the .docx211–460 ms Unzipping and walking the document. The single largest deterministic cost.
  3. 03Split into questions~1 ms Label detection over the block list.
  4. 04Detect the client format<1 ms Structural signals only — no model, despite what the name suggests.
  5. 05Parse every question5–11 ms All 245 questions across the three documents. This is the part that scales.
  6. 06Reconciliation pre-pass25 s – 12 min One whole-document model call to catch questions the splitter missed. Highly variable; the dominant cost on a good day and a liability on a bad one.
  7. 07Ask about what is left3–13 s each ~36% of questions. A 263-token prompt asking only for the missing field.

What that means for one 90-question document

Deterministic work: about one second. Model work: roughly 32 questions × ~6 s, plus the pre-pass — so 4 to 15 minutes end to end, essentially all of it waiting on a model.

The same document with its conventions fully taught would finish in about two seconds. That is the whole argument for the teaching loop: it is not a nicety, it is the difference between minutes and seconds per document.

Reads are not the constraint. Opening a project is a single indexed row read of stored JSON — extraction never re-runs when a project is selected, which was already the case and is confirmed here at 1.0 ms regardless of how many projects exist.

Storage is the constraint. A 73-question definition is ~190 KB, so 2,000 projects is 376 MB. The free Neon tier is 0.5 GB, which is roughly 2,600 projects before it has to be paid for or trimmed.

The real scaling risk is concurrent uploads, not project count

Each upload starts its own unbounded background thread. Ten researchers uploading at once means ten threads competing for the same rate-limited model quota, each slowing the others and none of them queued or prioritised. Nothing caps this today.

The fix is a small work queue with a concurrency limit — two or three extractions at a time, the rest waiting visibly. Worth doing before more than a couple of people use it at once.

Accuracy claims that were wrong until today

Two bugs found this session mean earlier confidence was misplaced, and both are worth knowing before the tool is shown to anyone.

Screen-outs fired on nobody. The codes were written to the wrong field, so 16 of the 17 terminate conditions across the three documents matched no respondent — in evaluation and in the fielded survey — while the review screen showed an empty codes box. Fixed and now asserted by running the conditions, not by inspecting their shape.

Randomisation was detected and dropped. 60 questions said randomise and were being fielded in fixed order. Fixed.

Data phase

Run against a real client file

The Google Ads questionnaire was checked against its own export: 750 respondents, 440 columns, a companion data map. It took 1.6 seconds. Two defects surfaced that no test had caught.

The data map was missing 48% of the file

A multi-select question is written in the data map as an unbracketed parent whose columns are listed underneath, in the second column: [DA_GOALSr1] | Generate new customer leads. Only the [NAME]: label form was recognised, so every punch column was skipped — 189 of 440 columns, and nearly every multi-select question in the study. A one-character difference in the convention was the entire cause. Coverage is now 440 of 440, with no column left undescribed.

The cross-check reported 715 findings on good data

It judged answers against the extracted option list and emitted one finding per distinct bad value — 14 of them for ages 32 to 45, against a question the extractor had mis-read as coded. All perfectly good data.

The data map is now the authority on what a column holds: an open numeric is never code-checked, and all bad values for a question are one finding. Same file, 715 → 7 findings, every one true — including three open-end questions the extractor had wrongly read as coded.

Techniques

What the tool now understands about MR

Read structurally from the directives, every pattern taken verbatim from the three client questionnaires.

Masking, piping and least fill — 13 questions across the three documents declare one.
TechniqueWritten asUnderstood as
Masking — only selected[PN: ONLY DISPLAY OPTIONS SELECTED AT S6]options ← S6, selected only
Masking — rows[PN: SHOW ONLY ITEMS SELECTED AT Q18 IN ROWS]rows ← Q18
Masking — whole list[SHOW Q26 LIST]options ← Q26
Masking — minus a pick[SHOW Q26 LIST EXCEPT FOR Q27 SELECTION]Q26 minus the Q27 answer
Masking — option levelUnaware (Not selected at Q2a)this option only if not chosen at Q2a
Piping — from a question[INSERT RESPONSE FROM Q8]Q8’s answer into the wording
Piping — scripter variable[INSERT PHARMACY]recorded by name, never guessed
Least fillASSIGN BASED ON LEAST FILLversion balanced by fill

Two distinctions made on purpose. A pipe is not a mask — INSERT RESPONSE FROM Q8 puts one answer into wording; INSERT BRANDS … FROM Q13 builds an option list, and confusing them claims a question’s options come from somewhere they don’t. The most specific mask wins — SHOW Q26 LIST EXCEPT FOR Q27 SELECTION also matches the plain form, and reading it plainly would show a respondent the option they just picked.

The payoff is a check nothing else can do: a masked question may only hold codes the respondent chose at the source. A mis-scripted mask shows the wrong list to real people and leaves no other trace in the output.

Fielding

Two defects that made a link unusable

Every respondent was screened out on their first answer

A negated screen-out — “TERMINATE if 1 not selected”, stored as not(in …) — is true against an empty answer set: the inner test is false, and not false is true. Every question’s terminate was judged on every submit, including questions nobody had reached. A screen-out is now only judged once the questions it references have actually been answered — the rule the quota check already used three lines below.

Scripter instructions were being shown to respondents

CODE AGE — BASED ON RESPONSES AT S1 is an instruction to the programmer. Derived variables now never render, while genuine intro and transition text still does. They also no longer count toward “N of M approved”, which was making the review queue unreachable by including items nobody can act on.

Routing kept as prose is not applied in the field

When extraction cannot resolve “ASK IF AWARE OF BRAND AT Q4” into a question and codes, it keeps the sentence for a reviewer. A sentence cannot be evaluated, so that question is shown to every respondent. Creating a link now names those questions instead of letting it pass silently — the questionnaire looks routed, the data comes back wrong, and nothing in between used to say so.

Waves

Only reading what changed

The pieces exist and are not yet joined up. Questionnaires are already versioned per study, and there is a working diff that compares two extractions question by question — it is what the correction preview uses to show “41 improved, 6 regressed” before a rule is approved. Wave-over-wave comparison of the data is fully built in the tabs engine.

What is missing is the wiring: re-uploading a revised client document creates a new version and re-reads it from scratch instead of diffing against the approved one. Joining those is a contained piece of work, and it makes the second wave of a tracker cheap in exactly the way the teaching loop makes the second client document cheap.

One deliberate caution: the diff labels a field that changed on both sides as changed, never as an improvement. A rule that rewrites forty question texts into different wrong text would look like a triumph under a naive metric; here it reads as forty things to look at.

Recommendation

What to do next, in order

  • Cap concurrent extractions. A small queue, two or three at a time. This is the one item that bites as soon as more than one person uses the tool.
  • Report rule-vs-model per document. The cost is genuinely falling as founders teach it, but nobody can see that without running a script. Make it a number on the project page.
  • Lift extraction coverage on spec-table documents. 24 of 75 questions on the Google Ads file is what caps everything downstream — the cross-check can only judge what was extracted.
  • Wire re-upload to the diff. Turns a wave revision into a short review instead of a full re-read.
  • Route questionnaires through the orchestrator so the two halves of the system share one entry point.

One thing that does not need doing: storing extracted questions so they are not re-read on every open. That was already the case, and it is confirmed above at 1.0 ms to open a project at any scale tested.
