The problem

Scripting is the slowest, riskiest step in every study

Someone reads forty pages and rebuilds them by hand in a survey tool — every answer list, every "ask only if", every screen-out, mask and pipe. It takes days, it caps how many studies a team can run, and its mistakes are found in field: a base pointing at the wrong question, a screen-out that fires on everyone, a brand list showing brands the respondent never named. By then the sample has been paid for.

The industry's sharper problem is trust. Data quality is the obstacle researchers name most often, and fast AI tools that cannot show their work make it worse. DVV Research Cloud is built to be fast and provably right.

What it does

From document to delivery, in one system

Reads the questionnaire

Word or PDF, prose or spec tables. Questions, types, answer codes, bases, screen-outs, grids, loops, carousels, masks, pipes, rotation and hidden variables — each with the line it came from. Rules read most of it with no AI at all; an open-weight model handles only the residue.

Takes a second look

Every flagged question is revisited with the whole questionnaire in view, fixed by rule where the document settles it, or by a model shown the questions it names. A model may only use the document's own wording. The reviewer gets one plain sentence per question and a button: undo, apply, or decide.

Learns your house style

A reviewer teaches a convention once — "in this client's documents, this phrase means select all" — and it applies to the whole document and every future one in that format.

Runs the survey

A phone-first link where screen-outs, quotas, masks, pipes and rotation are decided on the server, never in the browser. Panel redirects, soft launch, device and place paradata with the IP hashed.

Checks fielded data

Questionnaire plus data export — from this system or any other platform — and it finds, respondent by respondent, everyone who broke the questionnaire's rules.

Delivers

Labelled SPSS with its codebook, Excel, weighted crosstabs with significance letters, verbatim coding, an Excel deck, a client share link, and a copilot that acts only through cards a person confirms.

Before a respondent sees it

Four gates stand between a mistake and the field

Each catches what the others miss. A survey cannot go live while a blocking issue is open.

1 · Review

One sentence per issue

Unclear rules are shown with their reason and a recommended fix. Approval is blocked until they are settled.

2 · Dry run

Synthetic respondents

Hundreds of simulated people walk every path: conditions that never fire, questions nobody can reach.

3 · Pre-link check

Server refuses bad links

The finished instrument is re-checked on the server. Overriding a failure is possible, and logged.

4 · Soft launch

Hold at the first completes

The link pauses after the first interviews so real data is checked before full field.

Document codes and script codes are matched by answer wording, so a reversed or renumbered scale is never mistaken for bad data. Against an independent audit of the same files, 6 of 9 findings matched with the same respondents; the other 3 traced to the reference reading or the document.

The second look took flagged questions across the six documents from 106 to 80 — and every one left now carries a plain reason and a next step. Many remaining differences come from a few open product decisions — required by default, keeping "Other" last when the document is silent — rather than misreadings. Why it can be trusted

Four decisions that are hard to copy

Rules before AI

Market-research conventions are parsed, not guessed. Same document, same answer, every time — with a line to point at when someone asks why.

It says what it could not read

A guessed base is worse than none. Anything unclear reaches a person with its reason and a recommended fix, never silently.

It gets better with every client

Each correction can become a taught convention. The tool that reads a client's tenth study is measurably better than the one that read their first.

Data stays private

One private stack per client. Open-weight models only, on AWS Bedrock — no OpenAI or Anthropic models in the product, no client document sent to free AI services.

What we need

Three decisions, in order

  1. One real study to run end to end — a live project with a deadline, so the last gaps show where we can fix them.
  2. The product defaults — required unless stated, and "Other" kept last when silent. Two choices that close most of the remaining differences.
  3. Who owns approval — the system replaces scripting, not judgement. Someone signs off that the survey is what the client asked for.
