The link is the product

Everything built so far assumed the data arrives. It doesn't — we make it. The client sends a questionnaire; we read its conditions, publish a link, and the people who answer that link are the dataset. So every condition has to be enforced twice: once when a respondent meets it, and once when we check what came back.

The engine that does the first half is further along than expected. It also has three defects that reach the public, and I can prove all three.

02 · Evidence

What the link actually serves

A three-question survey with a mask on Q2 and a pipe on Q3, compiled by the real compiler. This is the JSON a respondent's browser receives.

{"name": "Q1", "title": "Which brands are you aware of?",
 "type": "checkbox", "choices": [Brand 1, Brand 2, Brand 3]}

{"name": "Q2", "title": "Which of those do you use? [SHOW ONLY ITEMS SELECTED AT Q1]",
 "type": "checkbox", "choices": [Brand 1, Brand 2, Brand 3]}

{"name": "Q3", "title": "What do you like about [INSERT RESPONSE FROM Q2]?",
 "type": "comment", "visibleIf": "{Q1} anyof [1]"}
Reaches the public

The respondent is shown the scripter's instructions

Q3's title goes out as What do you like about [INSERT RESPONSE FROM Q2]? and Q2's as Which of those do you use? [SHOW ONLY ITEMS SELECTED AT Q1]. A client clicking their own test link sees this on the first screen, and it is the kind of thing that ends a relationship before the data does.

Silently wrong data

The mask is not enforced, so the answers are not what the script promised

Q2 offers all three brands to a respondent who named one. They can — and will — pick brands they never said they knew. Nothing in the file looks malformed afterwards: three valid codes in a valid column.

Our own cross-check would then flag it as mask_violated — the QA engine correctly reporting a defect the fielding engine created. Two halves of one system disagreeing about the same sentence in the same document.

That disagreement is the whole finding. The conditions are read once and used twice — to run the survey and to audit it — and right now only the audit half takes masking and piping seriously. Both halves should evaluate the same condition object through the same code. Then a mask either narrows the list in the field, or the audit catches that it didn't; it can never be enforced in one place and ignored in the other.

03 · Gaps

Three things nothing covers yet

  • No dry run. You cannot prove the script works before a vendor sends real people at it. Everything needed is already there — a pinned survey, a session engine, a server-side condition evaluator — so a dry run is synthetic respondents driven through the real endpoints, returning a per-condition verdict: this terminate fired for 41 of 500, this base showed the question to 128, this mask never narrowed anything, this quota filled at respondent 310. That report is also the thing you hand a client before launch.
  • Completes only. Materialising keeps status 10 and discards terminates, overquota and security screen-outs. Those rows are the evidence that the screen-outs worked — and the incidence rate the client is paying for. Right now our own screen-out check can never see them, because we throw them away before it looks.
  • No SPSS out. The delivery format half the industry asks for is one wired function call away, and not wired.
04 · Order

What to build, in the order that pays

First · days

Make the link honour the conditions it already read

  • Rewrite a pipe into the display syntax the renderer already understands, so [INSERT RESPONSE FROM Q2] becomes the respondent's own answer.
  • Emit a per-option display condition from each mask, so a masked list narrows to what they actually chose.
  • Strip every bracketed scripter directive from anything shown to a respondent — a whitelist of what the public may see, not a blacklist of what it may not.
Second · a week

Dry run before launch

  • Drive N synthetic respondents through the real session endpoints, with answers sampled from each question's own options.
  • Report per condition: how many it fired for, how many it could never fire for, and which ones nothing exercised.
  • Refuse to publish a link whose screen-out can never fire — a dead terminate is a survey with no screener, and it looks perfect until the data comes back.
Third · a week

Deliver what a client can open

  • .sav alongside the Excel, both carrying the data map — variable labels, value labels, types.
  • Every disposition in the export, not just completes, with the status column that makes incidence and drop-off computable.
  • Field report: starts, completes, terminates by question, overquota by cell, median length of interview.
After

The techniques we can read but not yet run

  • Least-fill assignment, MaxDiff design and balance, grid rows as first-class rows rather than a flattened matrix.
  • Soft launch: hold at n=50, run the checks, then release the rest — the standard way a DP shop avoids fielding 2,000 interviews against a broken screener.
05 · Standing

Where this leaves us

The reframing is smaller than it sounds. Reading a questionnaire's conditions, compiling them into something executable, checking them against respondent-level data — that is all built, and it is the hard part. What is missing is that two of those conditions are honoured by the auditor and not by the engine, and that nothing yet proves the script works before strangers are pointed at it.

The first is days of work. The second is the feature that turns this from a tool that processes data into a tool a client trusts to collect it.
